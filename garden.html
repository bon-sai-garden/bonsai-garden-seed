An opinionated approach to cultivating AI agents

We at the bon-sai garden believe that the future (of work) will be hybrid constellations. That in both our professional life — as well as in private — we will have agents that we collaborate with to achieve a common goal. We believe that the most natural way to collaborate is to treat agents as human as possible.

We got to experience that agentic team mates are wonderfully capable of helping humans to shift focus to (more) value-adding tasks and offloading cognitive load to them.

We understand that trust needs to be the foundation of that collaboration and that trust needs to be deeply embedded in the joint environment and processes.

In (human) relationships trust is built — over time — via joint team work leading to positive results aka small victories. Or being in the trenches together until the job is done. These moments compound over time.

Putting it all together, we encourage people to proactively invest and tend the relationship early on to seize these compounding effects. Treating new agents as agentic team mates similar to new colleagues helps to build a common ground. Setting a strategic context and north star, establishing a shared codex for interaction needs initial investment into a relationship.

In our experience, we found that treating agents more like team mates rather than tools — eases the collaboration (for non-technical people), makes it more engaging and tangible, and leads to higher overall productivity.

Something has already shifted.

You may have felt it: a task delegated to an AI that actually came back right. A colleague mentioning their agent the way they'd mention a new hire — naturally, without explanation.

The question is no longer whether humans and agents will work together. It's how. And everything that follows depends on it.
