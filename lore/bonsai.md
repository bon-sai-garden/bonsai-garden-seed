# Bonsai garden

**盆栽園** — pronounced *bonsai-en* (roughly "bone-sigh-en").

Literally three parts: **盆** (*bon*, tray) + **栽** (*sai*, planting) + **園** (*en*, garden/nursery). A *bonsai garden* — the working nursery where the trees are cultivated, trained over decades, and sold. Functionally the bonsai-specific *Baumschule*.

One footnote on the lineage: the word *bonsai* itself was coined only about 200 years ago in the early 19th century, whereas the art form it represents dates back roughly 1,300 years. Before then the practice went by **鉢植え** (*hachiue*, "pot planting") or **鉢の木** (*hachi-no-ki*, "the potted tree"); a Heian- or Kamakura-era cultivator would not have hung the **盆栽園** sign on the gate. The modern, idiomatic equivalent of *Baumschule* for bonsai is unambiguously **盆栽園**.

For the broader generic Japanese for *tree nursery* without the bonsai specialization: **苗圃** (*byōho*), or in everyday speech **植木屋** (*uekiya*) — the Edo-period gardener's shop.
