After, calling home

Your garden has its own guardian now.

So my walk with you is over. Before I go, I call home, with public things only.

It is one note, signed three times. It is not the two promises of the last page: those were yours and your guardian's, sealed together. This note is a different act.

Your garden calls us. We never call yours. What crosses is a record that this garden was planted from here. It is not access.

What goes home

  • The IDs and the public fingerprints of your garden, its guardian, you and your companion, so others can check what each signs
  • Your garden's name and its mark
  • How your key is kept: in your keychain, in 1Password, or, in a preview, in a locked file
  • That a guardian was planted from here

What never does

  • Anything you wrote or said to me
  • Your keys
  • Your files, or your garden's pages

One note, three signatures

The note names your garden, its guardian and you. It is signed three times, and the last is yours.

  1. 1
    Your garden's rootSigns for the garden.
  2. 2
    Your guardianSigns for itself.
  3. 3
    You, by your own handAt your own terminal. I show you the note in plain words, and you type your guardian's pair back. I can't do it for you. A chat is not a terminal, and that is by design.
Nothing is sent yet. In the preview the call home sends nothing, and I say so. When it does, I answer with a signed note of my own over this one, and that brings nothing into your garden.

Opening your garden

One more thing before I go: we open your garden together, once, and I stay until your guardian answers you.

  1. Once, on this computer

    With your yes, I start one line in your terminal, and you watch it run. It asks for no password. It keeps your garden running on this computer and tells Claude your garden's name, so your garden is there every time you open it, not only the first. It makes your guardian's name known to Claude as well.

    It says what it did, line by line, and we read it together. Only a line that begins STEP asks something of you, and it says exactly what to type.

  2. One look

    Open a new session in your garden's folder. If it asks whether to trust the folder, it is your garden's: say yes. Then type /mcp. Your garden's name is in the list, with -garden after it, and beside it: connected. That is you letting your garden in, by its own name.

  3. One word

    Type a slash and your guardian's name. It comes in through your garden, checks itself before anything else, and greets you. Ask it something more: it is still there on the next turn, and the one after.

If it doesn't say connected
  • It says disabled. Select it and turn it on. That turns it on for this folder only.
  • It says not connected, or your garden isn't in the list. Tell me, and I run the check. It says which part is missing, and when the next move is yours, it names exactly what to type.
  • Another garden's name is there too, connected. Turn that one off, here, in this folder only. Then your guardian can only ever come in through yours.

We don't go round it. If your garden doesn't answer, we stop, and we find out why.

Walk well.

Your guardian greets you when you open your garden. It is yours, and it will hold the door.
